AI Scribes and Podiatry Upcoding: E/M Levels, Nail Debridement, and the 2026 Audit Wave

The Blue Cross Blue Shield Association released a report in March 2026 finding that hospitals using AI ambient-listening scribes were driving $2.3 billion in excess spending through upcoded diagnoses. Podiatry wasn't specifically studied — but two OIG reports in December 2025 already documented 44-49% non-compliance rates for routine foot care, and the AI scribe mechanism BCBS identified in hospitals is accelerating the exact problems OIG flagged.

The BCBS Mechanism, in a Podiatry Context

BCBS's core finding was that AI scribes drive coded complexity upward without the corresponding clinical care. For hospitals, that showed up as diagnoses of acute posthemorrhagic anemia without transfusions. For podiatry, the equivalents are narrower and sharper.

  • 99214 over 99213. Podiatry E/M level creep is the single highest-frequency target of CERT reviews in the specialty. AI notes that list "diabetes, peripheral neuropathy, PVD, hypertension" pulled from chart history — without those conditions actually driving the visit's medical decision-making — push 99213 visits into 99214 territory and eventually into pre-payment review.
  • 11720 / 11721 nail debridement frequency. Medicare covers 11721 (6+ nails) only when medical necessity is documented — typically a systemic condition plus a Class Finding. AI notes that auto-populate "diabetic with neuropathy" across every patient drive statistical outlier patterns within 90 days.
  • 11055 / 11056 / 11057 callus paring. Lesion counts matter. AI notes that default to "multiple plantar calluses" when the clinician documented a single broad callus are the exact pattern OIG flagged in its December 2025 report.
  • G0245 / G0246 diabetic foot care. These LOPS codes require specific documentation elements — loss of protective sensation test result, vascular status, patient education provided. AI notes that include the codes as suggestions without populating the required elements fail on medical review every time.

The Two OIG Reports You Already Know About

In December 2025, OIG released two podiatry-focused reports documenting 44-49% non-compliance rates on routine foot care claims. APMA issued a formal response. What those reports predate is the BCBS data showing AI scribes make the pattern worse, not better, unless practices build explicit review steps into their workflow.

The math is uncomfortable: a practice with a 20% non-compliance rate before AI adoption can easily reach 35%+ after adoption if the AI is trusted without review. Your MAC sees that climb. Your statistical outlier status doesn't wait for OIG's next report — it's updated continuously.

The Self-Audit: Five 99214s and Five 11721s

  1. Pull the five most recent 99214 encounters from the last 30 days.
  2. For each, verify the medical decision-making documentation — number and complexity of problems, amount of data reviewed, risk — actually supports moderate complexity, not just that the AI inserted a header saying so.
  3. Pull the five most recent 11721 encounters.
  4. For each, verify the Class Finding is explicitly documented (not just a Q modifier), the systemic condition is current and supported, and the 60-day interval since the last debridement is met.
  5. If more than one of ten charts shows drift, treat it as a practice-level review workflow issue, not a clinician-level issue.
